Research Proposal

Project Title: Developing a Novel Approach to Sustainable Urban Farming in Underserved Communities

Principal Investigator: Dr. Emily Greenfield, Department of Environmental Studies, Rockville University

Funding Agency: United States Department of Agriculture (USDA)

1. Introduction and Background
Urban farming has emerged as a viable solution to address food insecurity and promote sustainable food systems in underserved communities. However, existing approaches often face challenges related to limited access to land, resources, and technical expertise. This research proposal aims to develop a novel, community-driven approach to urban farming that addresses these barriers and empowers local residents to take an active role in transforming their neighborhoods into thriving, self-sustaining food hubs.

2. Objectives and Research Questions
The primary objective of this project is to design and implement a comprehensive urban farming model that is tailored to the unique needs and resources of underserved communities. The key research questions to be addressed are:
1. What are the specific challenges and barriers faced by underserved communities in accessing and maintaining urban farming initiatives?
2. How can local knowledge, partnerships, and community engagement be leveraged to create a sustainable and scalable urban farming model?
3. What technological and educational interventions can be implemented to support urban farmers and ensure the long-term viability of the urban farming initiatives?

3. Methodology
This project will employ a mixed-methods approach, combining qualitative and quantitative data collection and analysis techniques. The research will be conducted in three phases:

Phase 1: Community Assessment and Needs Analysis
The research team will conduct a comprehensive assessment of the target communities to identify the specific challenges, resources, and opportunities related to urban farming. This will include focus group discussions, interviews with local stakeholders, and a survey of community members to gather both subjective and objective data.

Phase 2: Design and Implementation of the Urban Farming Model
Based on the findings from Phase 1, the research team will work closely with community members to co-design and implement a tailored urban farming model. This will involve securing land access, providing technical training and support, and establishing an infrastructure for sustainable food production and distribution.

Phase 3: Evaluation and Dissemination
The research team will monitor and evaluate the impact of the urban farming initiatives, tracking indicators such as food production, income generation, and community engagement. The findings will be disseminated through academic publications, community outreach events, and policy briefs to inform and inspire similar efforts in other underserved areas.

4. Project Timeline and Budget
The proposed project will be carried out over a period of 3 years, with the following timeline and budget:

Year 1: Community Assessment and Model Design ($200,000)
- Conduct needs assessment and community engagement activities ($75,000)
- Secure land access and infrastructure development ($100,000)
- Develop educational and technical training materials ($25,000)

Year 2: Implementation and Evaluation ($300,000)
- Implement urban farming initiatives and provide technical support ($200,000)
- Monitor and evaluate project outcomes ($50,000)
- Establish community-based distribution and marketing channels ($50,000)

Year 3: Dissemination and Scaling ($150,000)
- Analyze and publish research findings ($50,000)
- Organize community events and stakeholder workshops ($50,000)
- Develop a scalable model and secure additional funding sources ($50,000)

Total Budget: $650,000

6. Conclusion
This research proposal outlines a comprehensive and community-driven approach to addressing the challenges of urban farming in underserved communities. By leveraging local knowledge, partnerships, and innovative technological solutions, the project aims to create a sustainable and scalable urban farming model that can be replicated in other similar contexts. The findings from this research will contribute to the growing body of knowledge on sustainable and equitable food systems, with the ultimate goal of improving food security and promoting community resilience.
