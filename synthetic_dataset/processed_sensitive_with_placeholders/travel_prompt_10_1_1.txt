Country Overview

Guatemala is a nation located in Central America, bordered by Mexico, Belize, Honduras, and El Salvador. With its diverse landscapes, rich cultural heritage, and ancient Mayan ruins, it is a popular destination for adventure seekers, history buffs, and nature enthusiasts alike.

The country boasts a stunning array of natural wonders, including the majestic volcanoes of Antigua, the pristine lakes of Lake Atitlán, and the lush rainforests of Petén. The ancient city of Tikal, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is a must-visit for those interested in Mayan civilization, offering a glimpse into the grandeur of this once-mighty empire.

Guatemala City, the capital, is a bustling metropolis that blends modernity with colonial charm. Visitors can explore the vibrant markets, savor the rich flavors of Guatemalan cuisine, and immerse themselves in the local culture through music, art, and festivals.

While Guatemala offers a wealth of experiences, it is essential for travelers to be aware of the potential risks and take necessary precautions. The following sections provide important information to ensure a safe and enjoyable trip.

Safety and Security

Guatemala faces challenges related to crime and civil unrest. Travelers should exercise caution, especially in urban areas and regions with a history of gang activity or drug trafficking. It is advisable to avoid carrying valuable items, wearing expensive jewelry, or displaying cash in public.

Petty crimes such as pickpocketing and bag snatching are common, particularly in crowded areas and on public transportation. Travelers should be vigilant and keep their belongings close at all times.

Political demonstrations and protests can occur without warning, and some have turned violent in the past. Visitors should monitor local news and avoid areas where large gatherings or protests are taking place.

In rural areas, travelers should exercise caution when hiking or exploring remote locations. It is recommended to hire a licensed guide or join organized tours to mitigate risks and ensure safety.

For emergencies or assistance, travelers can contact the Embassy or Consulate, or dial the emergency numbers provided in the Embassy Contacts section of this advisory.

Health Information

Travelers to Guatemala should be aware of potential health risks and take appropriate precautions. It is recommended to consult a healthcare professional or visit a travel health clinic before the trip to receive any necessary vaccinations or medications.

Mosquito-borne illnesses such as dengue fever, chikungunya, and Zika virus are present in Guatemala. Travelers should use insect repellent, wear protective clothing, and consider staying in accommodations with air conditioning or screened windows.

Water and food-borne illnesses are also a concern, especially in remote areas. Visitors should drink only bottled or purified water and avoid consuming raw or undercooked food.

Altitude sickness can affect travelers visiting high-altitude destinations like Antigua or the highlands. It is advisable to allow time for acclimatization and seek medical attention if experiencing severe symptoms.

Travelers should purchase comprehensive travel insurance that includes coverage for medical emergencies and evacuation, if necessary.

Entry and Exit Requirements

Visitors to Guatemala must have a valid passport with at least six months of remaining validity beyond the intended stay. Citizens of certain countries, including the United States, Canada, and most European nations, do not require a visa for stays of up to 90 days. However, it is essential to check the specific entry requirements for your nationality before travel.

To register your travel plans online, you will need to create an account on the government's travel registration website. Upon arrival in Guatemala, travelers may be required to provide proof of sufficient funds for their stay, a return or onward ticket, and a confirmed accommodation reservation.
