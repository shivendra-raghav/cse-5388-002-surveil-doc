Data Governance Policy

Introduction
Effective data governance is essential for our organization to ensure the quality, security, and appropriate use of data assets. This Data Governance Policy outlines the principles, roles, and responsibilities for managing data across the enterprise. The goal is to establish a framework that promotes data as a strategic asset, protects sensitive information, and enables data-driven decision making.

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and third parties who access, use, or manage data owned or controlled by our organization. Adherence to this policy is mandatory, and failure to comply may result in disciplinary action.

Data Classification
Proper data classification is the foundation of our data governance program. All data assets must be categorized according to their level of sensitivity and potential impact. The following data classification levels will be used:

Public Data: Information that is intended for public consumption and does not require any access restrictions. Examples include marketing materials, press releases, and publicly available reports.

Internal Data: Confidential information that is intended for internal use only. This may include financial records, HR documents, and operational data. Access to internal data is restricted to authorized personnel.

Sensitive Data: Information that requires a high degree of protection due to legal, regulatory, or operational considerations. This includes personally identifiable information (PII), protected health information (PHI), payment card data, and other sensitive data types. Access to sensitive data is tightly controlled and monitored.

All employees are responsible for understanding the classification of the data they work with and applying the appropriate security controls. Data owners, in collaboration with the Data Governance Council, are responsible for defining and maintaining the data classification scheme.

Data Access Control
Access to data assets must be granted based on the principle of least privilege. Users should only be provided the minimum permissions necessary to perform their job functions. The following access control measures will be implemented:

Authentication: All users must authenticate with a unique username and strong password to access data systems. Multi-factor authentication will be required for sensitive data access.

Authorization: User permissions will be reviewed and updated on a regular basis. Access privileges will be revoked promptly when an employee's role changes or their employment is terminated.

Logging and Monitoring: All access attempts, both successful and unsuccessful, will be logged. The logs will be regularly reviewed for suspicious activity.

Data Owners, in collaboration with the IT Department, are responsible for defining and implementing appropriate access control measures for their respective data assets.

Data Quality
Maintaining high-quality data is essential for informed decision-making and operational efficiency. The following data quality principles will be applied:

Accuracy: Data must be correct, complete, and free from errors. Data stewards will implement validation checks and data reconciliation processes to ensure accuracy.

Timeliness: Data must be up-to-date and reflective of the current state of the business. Processes will be in place to ensure timely data updates and synchronization.

Completeness: Data must be comprehensive and include all necessary information to support business requirements. Data stewards will define and enforce data completeness standards.

Consistency: Data must be standardized and coherent across the organization. Data stewards will establish data dictionaries, taxonomies, and other standards to ensure consistency.

Data Owners, in collaboration with the Data Governance Council, are responsible for defining and enforcing data quality standards for their respective data assets.

Data Retention
Our organization must comply with various legal, regulatory, and operational requirements regarding the retention and disposal of data. The following data retention principles will be applied:

Compliance: Data will be retained in accordance with all applicable laws, regulations, and industry standards. The Data Governance Council will maintain a data retention schedule that outlines the required retention periods for different data types.

Business Need: Data will be retained for as long as it is necessary to support the organization's business operations and decision-making processes. Data Owners will regularly review their data assets and identify any that can be safely disposed of.

Secure Disposal: When data is no longer required, it will be securely disposed of using approved methods, such as shredding, degaussing, or secure data erasure. The IT Department will be responsible for implementing and monitoring data disposal processes.

Data Owners, in collaboration with the Data Governance Council and the Legal/Compliance Department, are responsible for defining and enforcing data retention policies for their respective data assets.

Roles and Responsibilities
Effective data governance requires a clear delineation of roles and responsibilities. The following key roles and their responsibilities are defined:

Data Owners: Individuals or teams responsible for the management and oversight of specific data assets. Data Owners are accountable for data classification, access control, quality, and retention for their respective data domains.

Data Stewards: Individuals or teams responsible for the day-to-day management of data assets. Data Stewards implement data governance policies and procedures, ensure data quality, and serve as the primary point of contact for data-related inquiries.

Data Governance Council: A cross-functional team responsible for the development, implementation, and maintenance of the organization's data governance framework. The Council sets policies, standards, and guidelines, and oversees the overall data governance program.

IT Department: Responsible for the implementation and maintenance of data management systems, including infrastructure, security, and access controls. The IT Department works closely with Data Owners and Data Stewards to ensure the effective and secure management of data assets.

All Employees: Responsible for understanding and adhering to the organization's data governance policies and procedures. Employees must handle data with care, report any data incidents or breaches, and participate in data governance training and awareness initiatives.

Policy Compliance and Enforcement
Adherence to this Data Governance Policy is mandatory for all employees, contractors, and third parties who access, use, or manage data owned or controlled by our organization. Failure to comply may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment or contractual relationship.

The Data Governance Council is responsible for monitoring compliance with this policy and implementing appropriate enforcement mechanisms. This may include regular audits, spot checks, and the investigation of reported policy violations.

Conclusion
Effective data governance is essential for our organization to unlock the full value of our data assets, protect sensitive information, and enable data-driven decision making. This Data Governance Policy, along with the supporting roles, responsibilities, and procedures, establishes a framework for ensuring the quality, security, and appropriate use of data across the enterprise.

All employees are expected to familiarize themselves with this policy and actively participate in our data governance program. By working together, we can leverage our data as a strategic asset and drive success for our organization.
