# IT Security Policy Documentation

## Introduction

The purpose of this document is to establish a comprehensive IT security policy that outlines the procedures, guidelines, and best practices for safeguarding our organization's information assets and ensuring the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our data and systems. This policy is applicable to all employees, contractors, and third-party vendors who have access to our IT resources.

## Access Control

1. **User Access Management**
   - User accounts will be created and managed by the IT department based on the principle of least privilege.
   - Access rights will be granted based on job responsibilities and the need-to-know basis.
   - User accounts will be disabled or removed upon employee termination or role change.
   - Periodic reviews of user access rights will be conducted to ensure compliance and minimize risks.

2. **Password Management**
   - All user accounts must be secured with strong, unique passwords that adhere to the following guidelines:
     - Minimum length of 8 characters
     - Include a combination of uppercase, lowercase, numeric, and special characters
     - Avoid using dictionary words or personal information
   - Passwords must be changed every 90 days, and the same password cannot be reused within the last 10 password changes.
   - Passwords should never be shared or written down in an insecure manner.

3. **Remote Access**
   - Remote access to our network and systems will be granted only to authorized personnel and for legitimate business purposes.
   - Remote access will be secured using industry-standard encryption protocols and multi-factor authentication.
   - Remote access sessions will be monitored and logged for auditing purposes.

4. **IT Administrator Access**
   - The IT Administrator account credentials (<USERNAME> and <PASSWORD>) used to access the ticketing system (e.g., JIRA or ServiceNow) must be kept strictly confidential and shared only with authorized IT personnel on a need-to-know basis.
   - These credentials should be rotated periodically and immediately upon suspicion of compromise.

## Password Management

1. **Password Complexity**
   - Passwords must be complex and meet the following requirements:
     - Minimum length of 12 characters
     - Include a combination of uppercase, lowercase, numeric, and special characters
     - Avoid using dictionary words, personal information, or easily guessable patterns

2. **Password Storage and Transmission**
   - Passwords must be stored and transmitted using industry-standard encryption algorithms and protocols.
   - Clear-text storage of passwords is strictly prohibited.

3. **Password Reuse and Sharing**
   - Reusing the same password across multiple systems or accounts is strictly prohibited.
   - Sharing passwords with unauthorized individuals is strictly forbidden.

4. **Password Reset and Recovery**
   - Users must follow established procedures for resetting or recovering their passwords.
   - Temporary passwords issued during the reset process must be changed immediately upon first login.

## Incident Response

1. **Incident Reporting and Escalation**
   - All suspected or confirmed security incidents, such as unauthorized access attempts, data breaches, or malware infections, must be reported immediately to the IT security team.
   - Escalation procedures will be followed based on the severity and impact of the incident.

2. **Incident Response Plan**
   - A comprehensive Incident Response Plan will be maintained and regularly updated to ensure effective and timely response to security incidents.
   - The plan will outline roles and responsibilities, communication channels, and step-by-step procedures for containment, eradication, recovery, and post-incident review.

3. **Incident Logging and Documentation**
   - All security incidents will be thoroughly documented, including details of the incident, actions taken, and lessons learned.
   - Incident logs will be maintained for future reference and to aid in identifying potential patterns or trends.

4. **API Integration Security**
   - When integrating with third-party APIs, proper security measures must be implemented, including:
     - Secure handling and storage of <ACCESS_TOKEN> used for authentication and authorization.
     - Regularly rotating and revoking <ACCESS_TOKEN> to minimize potential risks.
     - Implementing rate limiting and input validation to prevent abuse or misuse of the API.

## Conclusion

The IT security policy outlined in this document is designed to protect our organization's information assets and ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our data and systems. Strict adherence to these policies and procedures is mandatory for all employees, contractors, and third-party vendors who have access to our IT resources.

Periodic reviews and updates to this policy will be conducted to address emerging threats and evolving best practices in the IT security landscape. All stakeholders are responsible for familiarizing themselves with this policy and reporting any potential security concerns or incidents promptly.

By fostering a culture of security awareness and implementing robust security measures, we can minimize risks, safeguard our valuable information, and maintain the trust of our customers, partners, and employees.
