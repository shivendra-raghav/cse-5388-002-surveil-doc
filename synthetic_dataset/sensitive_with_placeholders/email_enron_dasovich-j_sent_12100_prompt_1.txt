Dear colleagues,

I wanted to bring to your attention an insightful article published in the San Francisco Chronicle regarding the current economic situation in California. The author, Michael J. Boskin, raises some valid concerns about the state's anti-business climate and the mishandling of the energy crisis by Governor Gray Davis.

One of the key points Boskin highlights is the governor's demagogic attacks on "outside" energy companies and the suggestion that California should go it alone. This rhetoric, coupled with the state's overtaxed and over-regulated environment, has contributed to the impression of an unfriendly business climate. Additionally, Boskin criticizes Davis' plan to have the state play an expanded role in energy production and transportation, questioning the state government's ability to make wise and efficient investment decisions.

Furthermore, Boskin argues that the initial refusal by Davis' appointees on the Public Utilities Commission to allow utilities to enter long-term contracts exacerbated the shortages and price spikes. The subsequent negotiation of long-term contracts at the peak of the market frenzy has locked the state into paying unnecessarily high prices. By keeping prices to consumers unrealistically controlled, the burden for energy costs has been passed on to taxpayers, both current and future.

Despite these concerns, Boskin remains optimistic about California's economic future, citing the state's ability to attract talent and foster innovation. However, he emphasizes the need for better state policies, including lower tax rates, less regulation, improved education, and more efficient energy and transportation infrastructure.
