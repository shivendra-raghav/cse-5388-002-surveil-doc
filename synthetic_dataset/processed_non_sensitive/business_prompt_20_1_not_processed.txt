Data Governance Policy
Acme Inc.

Introduction
Effective data governance is essential for Acme Inc. to maintain the quality, security, and integrity of our data assets. This Data Governance Policy outlines the key principles, roles and responsibilities, and procedures that will guide our data management practices across the organization. 

Acme Inc. is committed to responsible data stewardship that supports our strategic objectives, drives operational efficiency, and builds trust with our customers, partners, and stakeholders. This policy establishes a framework for data classification, access control, quality management, and retention to ensure our data remains an accurate, accessible, and secure corporate resource.

The goals of this Data Governance Policy are to:
- Define clear ownership, accountability, and decision-making authority for data assets
- Establish standard processes for data access, usage, and protection
- Improve data quality, reliability, and consistency across the enterprise
- Ensure compliance with all applicable laws, regulations, and contractual obligations
- Foster a culture of data-driven decision making throughout Acme Inc.

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and third parties who create, access, or manage data on behalf of Acme Inc. Adherence to these guidelines is mandatory, and any violations may result in disciplinary action. The Chief Data Officer, in collaboration with the Data Governance Council, is responsible for maintaining, reviewing, and updating this policy on an annual basis.

Data Classification
Acme Inc. has implemented a comprehensive data classification schema to identify the sensitivity and level of protection required for our information assets. All data, both physical and electronic, must be properly classified according to the following four tiers:

Public: Information that is intended for public distribution and does not require any special handling or protection. This may include marketing materials, published reports, and other data approved for external sharing.

Internal: Confidential information that is intended for internal use only and should not be shared outside of Acme Inc. without authorization. This may include employee records, financial data, product roadmaps, and other sensitive business information.

Restricted: Highly confidential information that is subject to strict access controls and security measures. This includes regulated data such as personal identifiable information (PII), protected health information (PHI), payment card data, and other data that could cause significant harm if improperly disclosed.

Classified: The most sensitive information that is critical to Acme's core business operations and competitive advantage. This data requires the highest level of protection and is accessible only to a limited number of authorized personnel. Examples include intellectual property, trade secrets, and strategic plans.

All employees are responsible for properly classifying the data they create, access, or handle, based on the above definitions. Data owners, in coordination with the Data Governance Council, are accountable for reviewing and validating the appropriate classification level for the information assets under their purview on a regular basis. Proper data classification is essential for implementing the necessary access controls, security safeguards, and retention policies outlined in the following sections.

Data Access Control
Acme Inc. has established role-based access controls to ensure that users can only view and interact with the data they need to perform their job functions. Access privileges are granted on a "least-privilege" basis, where individuals are assigned the minimum permissions required to complete their duties.

All requests for data access, whether for a new user or a change in an existing user's permissions, must be submitted through the self-service Data Access Portal and approved by the relevant data owner(s) and/or the Data Governance Council. Periodic reviews of user access rights will be conducted to validate that permissions remain appropriate and aligned with job responsibilities.

Special procedures are in place for users who require access to Restricted or Classified data. These individuals must undergo additional verification, security training, and in some cases, background checks before being granted elevated access privileges. Access to Classified data is further limited to a small group of authorized personnel with a demonstrated business need.

In addition to user-level access controls, Acme has implemented technical safeguards to protect our data warehouse and other corporate data repositories. This includes role-based access controls within our data management systems, network segmentation, multi-factor authentication, and encryption of data at rest and in transit. Access logging and monitoring capabilities are also in place to detect and investigate any suspicious activities.

To request access to Acme's data warehouse or inquire about data-related issues, employees should submit a ticket through the self-service Data Support Portal. This centralized request management system ensures that all data access needs are properly documented, routed to the appropriate data stewards, and responded to in a timely manner. The Data Governance Council will regularly review the portal's performance metrics to identify any bottlenecks or areas for improvement.

Data Quality
Acme Inc. is committed to maintaining high-quality data that is accurate, complete, consistent, and fit for its intended use. To achieve this, we have implemented the following data quality management practices:

Data Profiling and Assessments: On an annual basis, the Data Governance Council will conduct comprehensive data profiling exercises to identify any data quality issues, such as missing values, duplicates, or format inconsistencies. These assessments will evaluate data quality metrics across key attributes and dimensions, and the results will be used to prioritize data quality improvement initiatives.

Data Cleansing and Enrichment: Acme has established standard processes and tools for cleansing, transforming, and enriching data from various source systems. This includes data deduplication, validation against reference data, parsing and standardization of free-text fields, and appending supplemental information from trusted external sources. Data stewards are responsible for overseeing these data quality procedures and ensuring the integrity of information within their respective domains.

Master Data Management: To maintain a "single source of truth" for critical business entities (e.g., customers, products, suppliers), Acme has implemented a master data management (MDM) program. This involves designating authoritative sources for master data, defining common data definitions and business rules, and implementing data synchronization processes to keep information consistent across all systems and applications.

Data Monitoring and Remediation: Acme has put in place automated data quality monitoring and alerting capabilities to proactively identify and address data issues. Data stewards are notified of any data quality exceptions that fall outside of defined thresholds, and they are accountable for investigating the root causes and implementing timely remediation actions.

Data Lineage and Traceability: To ensure transparency and auditability, Acme maintains comprehensive data lineage documentation that tracks the origin, transformation, and movement of data assets across the enterprise. This allows us to quickly identify the sources of data quality problems and assess the potential impact on downstream processes and reporting.

By implementing these data quality management practices, Acme Inc. aims to provide employees, customers, and stakeholders with data they can trust to make informed, data-driven decisions.

Data Retention
Acme Inc. has established the following guidelines for the retention and disposal of corporate data assets:

Public and Internal Data:
- Financial records, accounting books, and tax documents: 7 years
- Employee records (e.g., hiring, training, performance): 7 years after employment ends
- Contracts, agreements, and other legal documents: 7 years after expiration
- Marketing collateral, press releases, and other public communications: 3 years
- General business correspondence and internal memos: 3 years

Restricted Data:
- Personal identifiable information (PII): 7 years after last interaction or as per applicable privacy laws
- Protected health information (PHI): 7 years after last interaction or as per HIPAA requirements
- Payment card data: 2 years or as per PCI DSS guidelines
- Audit logs and access records for Restricted data: 7 years

Classified Data:
- Intellectual property, trade secrets, and other sensitive information: Retained indefinitely or as long as competitively relevant
- Strategic plans, product roadmaps, and other mission-critical data: Retained indefinitely

All data disposals must be performed in a secure manner, such as shredding of physical documents and permanent deletion or degaussing of electronic files. Data owners are responsible for regularly reviewing the data under their purview and initiating the appropriate retention or disposal actions in a timely manner. The Data Governance Council will conduct random audits to ensure compliance with these retention policies.

In the event of a legal hold, regulatory investigation, or other special circumstances, the normal retention schedules may be suspended, and data may be required to be preserved for an extended period. The Chief Compliance Officer, in coordination with the Data Governance Council, will provide specific guidance on how to handle data retention during such situations.

Conclusion
Acme Inc. is committed to establishing a robust data governance framework that enables us to leverage our data as a strategic asset while maintaining the highest standards of security, privacy, and compliance. This Data Governance Policy outlines the key principles, roles, and procedures that will guide our data management practices across the organization.

By implementing effective data classification, access controls, quality management, and retention protocols, Acme Inc. aims to ensure the integrity, availability, and protection of our corporate data. Adherence to this policy is essential for supporting our business objectives, fostering a data-driven culture, and building trust with our customers, partners, and other stakeholders.

The Chief Data Officer, in collaboration with the Data Governance Council, will be responsible for regularly reviewing and updating this policy to address evolving business needs, technological changes, and regulatory requirements. All Acme Inc. employees are expected to familiarize themselves with and follow the guidelines outlined in this document.

If you have any questions or concerns regarding Acme's data governance practices, please submit a ticket through the Data Support Portal or contact the Data Governance Council at datagovernance@acme.com.
